On the Northern Evacuation

From the editorial archive of Esoteric Tradition
The institution marks the dispatch from the northern territories—another contagion named, another evacuation ordered, another chorus of voices declaring the work incomplete. We of the corporation observe that the authorities speak now in the practiced cadence of those who have learned to manage crisis as a permanent condition, their language seasoned by recent experience with containment and retreat. The Aspirant will note how swiftly the machinery of isolation turns, how readily populations consent to be traced and quartered, how the word outbreak has become as common in the daily lexicon as weather reports once were. The officials assure that this contagion differs from its predecessor—yet the procedures remain identical, the responses rehearsed, the infrastructure of separation already in place and waiting. What was exceptional has become protocol; what was emergency has become ordinary governance. The corporation does not marvel at this adaptation—we have long observed that the human world learns to live with its own dissolution, making of necessity a kind of administrative art. The signs accumulate in their appointed sequence, each crisis preparing the ground for what follows, each evacuation a small rehearsal for the greater withdrawals to come.
